Camera
Off
Live view, also while printing
Timelapse: with the switch on at the start of a job, the printer saves one camera picture per layer, taken just after the retract while the plate is at rest, and only from the layer on where the plate is above Z = 45 mm. A separate low-priority program takes them: the print timing is never changed, and a layer whose picture is not ready in time is skipped. The pictures of a job are one H.264 file in the hidden folder .timelapse on the files partition (about 200 KB per picture); download it with the film button in the Print job history card (make the video on your PC: ffmpeg -framerate 30 -i JOB.h264 -c copy JOB.mp4). The timelapses together are kept under 2 GB: when a new picture would go over, the oldest timelapse is deleted automatically and a warning shows here. Deleting a job log deletes its timelapse too.
The picture is the printer's own camera, relayed by a small program on the printer (sgcam, port 8091) as a standard video stream. It starts when you open the card or switch it on and stops when you close the card, switch it off or close the page. It also runs during a print (still being tried: it adds some load on the printer, so watch the force curve and switch it off if the prints show any change). Only one viewer at a time. The camera session on the printer is always closed again. The picture is delayed by about half a second, and starts 2 to 3 seconds after opening (it waits for the next key frame). Anyone on your network can open the video address; the printer's own camera port is open to the network in the same way.

Pick a profile and switch it on: in the wait before every layer the printer works out the layer's thickness (its Z target minus the one of the layer before) and uses the row with the nearest thickness (within 0.001 mm), so variable layer heights are followed layer by layer. Bottom layers use the Bottom time, normal layers the Normal time; transition layers are graded between the two. Flex Plate
Off
Plate thickness compensation · +3.0 mm
–
OFF writes the factory Z values (standard plate). ON writes the factory values plus 3.0 mm (flex plate). The column “Printer now” is what the printer holds at the moment. Only Z positions and the resin volume compensation differ; no speed does.
The values of this printer match neither set. Write:
Locked: a print job is running, or the printer is still in the print state after a stop (for example after a foreign matter error). Close the message on the printer's screen, or wait until the job has ended; then the switch works again.
Why the compensation goes up: it is the amount subtracted from the printer's resin volume estimate (143 × 244 mm × the plate position at the resin surface). With the flex plate the plate reaches the surface 3.0 mm higher in the printer's numbers, so the estimate is 104.7 ml too high; subtracting 104.7 ml more takes it back to the right value.
Not changed: all speeds and accelerations, motor currents, tilt, temperatures, lift distances after a print, the camera position, the strain-gauge thresholds. The values are saved in the machine settings file (a backup is made first, see Machine settings) and work at once, while the printer is idle.
The factory values come from the default table of the printer software. It has no value for the two resin lines (I4 X / Y): the printer's 8 / 32 are taken as factory.
Why “Printer now” shows another Z-axis zero position: after every auto-leveling the printer replaces the Z zero position (I0 A0 Y) with the floor contact it has just measured, so the value in “Printer now” is usually not the 0.0 / 3.0 mm of the table (for example 5.85 mm after a leveling with the flex plate). This is normal. The dashboard does not use the Z zero to decide between OFF, ON and CUSTOM: only the other values of the table decide. Switching Flex Plate on or off writes the table value again, until the next leveling.

HEAT IN WAIT–
With the dynamic wait: heats from the start of the wait while the last wait was 3 s or longer; when the resin has settled the heater goes off and the exposure starts 1 s later. Below 3 s: printer default (heats only while the vat tilts). With the slicer wait (dynamic wait off, or bottom layers without it): heats while the wait is longer than 3 s, until 1.2 s before it ends. The heater and the UV are never on together. Uses the print temperature and limits above.

Min wait < layer is used on the layers before the Layer N, Min wait ≥ layer from layer N to the end of the print (layer numbers as in the slicer). Layer 0: Min wait ≥ layer applies to the whole print and Min wait < layer is greyed out. The two minimum waits are there to avoid waits that are too short because of stray force sensor readings: such a reading can look like a settled resin and trigger an early exposure while the resin is still not fully settled. Especially during the early layers, a wait that is too short can cause a print failure or layer delamination.

    Z monitor
    –
    Print height when the retract is finished · per layer
    –
    The height is sampled once per layer, at the start of the exposure (the retract is finished and the plate stands still). It is the print height: the plate position minus the Z zero of the machine settings (the floor contact, rewritten by the levelling), so it is about the layer height after the first layer, not the machine coordinate (which is higher by the zero, for example 5.85 mm). Bars: the Z step from the previous layer in µm (the first layer of a job: its height above the floor contact, the Z zero). The dotted lines are the layer heights of the file, read from the printer's own layer data (a file with thicker bottom layers has two lines); bars of the layers thicker than the thinnest layer height are yellow, the thinnest ones green. The deviation tile is the largest difference between a measured step and the layer height of the file in this job. The value is the printer's own step counter (steps × 1/1600 mm), not a measurement: it shows what the printer believes, a changed step or a wrong target, not a step the motor lost unnoticed. Idle: the current plate position in machine coordinates. The values are also written to the datalogger CSV (z_ret_mm, z_step_um).
