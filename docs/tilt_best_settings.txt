BEST (user, dry run, by ear and by feel in the vat): MICROSTEP WAVE sin t - 0.09 sin 3t  ("seems a bit better", tiny steps felt less) on the otherwise STOCK driver
  (StealthChop on, IRUN 29 = the machine setting, CHOPCONF 0x100c3, PWMCONF 0x504c8). The opposite sign (+9 %) was clearly WORSE ("feeling more the steps"), +-3 % not audible.
  Apply (live, lost at power cycle; needs libsgtap with tiltreg, idle or between moves):
      TPW=<pw> tools/tiltreg.sh "wave -9"            (or the raw table: tools/tiltreg.sh "lut 0x00800000 0x20100200 0x42108410 0x44444444 0x04108844 0xefff0201 0x55b6ddef 0x008424a5 0xb0b0105a 0x00f70000")
      TPW=<pw> tools/tiltreg.sh stock                 (back to stock: also restores the default table)
      TPW=<pw> tools/tiltreg.sh cur                   (MSCNT, A, B at standstill; check with: python3 tools/mslut.py check -9 MSCNT A B)
  How the table works: tools/mslut.py (TMC5130 MSLUT0-7 0x60-0x67, MSLUTSEL 0x68, MSLUTSTART 0x69; f = sin t + a sin 3t, a = P/100, peak kept at 247 = the stock peak,
  so the peak current never rises; average torque and angles unchanged; a 4th-harmonic torque ripple of +-a is what can cancel the motor's full-step cogging).
  The app never writes the table (init writes GCONF, CHOPCONF, IHOLD_IRUN, PWMCONF, ramp regs only), so a boot-time write would have to come from libsgtap after the app's init
  (log ticks ~14.6 s uptime) and only at standstill (stst AND XACTUAL == XTARGET, as tiltreg does).
